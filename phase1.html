Phase 1: Needs Assessment

Identified problems

Students within senior high schools have become more integrated with digital technologies and use the internet for learning, entertainment, communication and other purposes. Many students still face difficulties when it comes to staying safe online, understanding fake information, cyber threats and using AI responsibly.

There is an increasing need to teach students how to be more digitally aware: identify AI-generated and human-generated content, spot fake information and online threats, and think critically about the content they interact with or share.

Survey questions

  • Hours online daily: 35.5% log 3–5 hours and 32.3% spend over 5 hours.
  • Personal device for schoolwork: 71% always have access.
  • Main internet use: school and research 45.2%, entertainment 29%, gaming 25.8%.
  • Digital tool skills: intermediate 45.2%, beginner 25.8%.
  • Biggest online problem: slow or unstable connections, 51.6%.
  • Schoolwork difficulty from connectivity/devices: 51.3% often or sometimes.
  • Cyberbullying, scams, unwanted contact: 38.7% know someone affected; 35.5% experienced it personally.
  • Parental monitoring: 38.7% report rare guidance.
  • Bedtime device use: frequently impacts sleep and class focus for 35.5%.
  • Confidence using AI responsibly: 41.9% somewhat confident, 35.5% not confident.

Key findings

1. Students are exposed to online safety risks

20 of 31 respondents (64.5%) or someone they know experienced cyberbullying, scams, unwanted contact, or other online problems.

2. Internet access remains a challenge

16 respondents (51.6%) named slow or unstable connections as their biggest online problem.

3. Technology affects daily routines

20 respondents (64.5%) said bedtime device use sometimes or often affects sleep or class concentration.

4. Students need confidence in responsible AI use

Only 13 (41.9%) were somewhat confident; 5 (16.1%) not confident; 2 (6.5%) do not use AI tools.

5. Access doesn't guarantee digital literacy

Although 22 respondents (71%) have personal devices, they still struggle with online safety and information verification.

Existing resources & gaps

What helps

MIL curriculum, CyberSafe orientations, smartphones, computer labs, school Wi-Fi, Canva, CapCut, and AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini).

Remaining gaps

Insufficient device-to-student ratios and outdated hardware · No stable internet and electricity in rural areas · Modules struggling to keep pace with AI deepfakes · Minimal technical support and home practice resources.

Proposed solution & impact

Project name: Beyond the Screen: Think before you click.

Scam Recognition

QR code challenges to spot phishing and suspicious messages.

Media Literacy

Evaluating real-world headlines to fight disinformation.

Ethical AI

Practicing responsible tool usage to avoid plagiarism.

Digital Wellness

Managing screen time and sleep schedules.

Expected impact: Empowers students with practical cybersecurity skills, fosters ethical AI habits, and creates a ripple effect of peer education across the community.
