Phase 4: Evaluation

Our Evaluation Survey

Summary

Access and usage

35.5% spend 3–5 hours a day and 32.3% more than 5 hours, so about two-thirds are online 3+ hours daily. 71% always have a personal device. School and research is the top use (45.2%), then entertainment (29%) and gaming (25.8%).

Skills and confidence

Most rate their digital tool skills intermediate (45.2%) or beginner (25.8%). On using AI responsibly, 41.9% feel somewhat confident and 35.5% are not confident.

Problems and risks

Slow or unstable internet is the biggest problem (51.6%). 51.3% have had trouble with schoolwork due to connectivity or devices. 35.5% experienced cyberbullying, scams or unwanted contact, and 38.7% know someone who has. 38.7% say parents rarely guide their tech use. 35.5% say late-night use affects sleep or focus.

Key insights

  • 1. Having a device doesn't mean having access. Over half still struggle with schoolwork because of unreliable internet. Connectivity, not hardware, is the real bottleneck.
  • 2. Heavy users, but not mainly for school. Entertainment and gaming together (about 55%) outweigh school and research.
  • 3. Online risks are widespread. Roughly 74% have some exposure to cyberbullying, scams or unwanted contact, worrying alongside low parental guidance.
  • 4. Skills and confidence lag behind usage. Digital literacy and AI-ethics training look like clear gaps.
  • 5. Sleep and focus are an overlooked cost. More than a third say late-night use hurts rest or class focus.
